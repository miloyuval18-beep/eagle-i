High-Value Area Permits
A list of real Houston homes where someone recently filed a building or renovation permit — a good sign they might need your services soon. Pulled fresh from the city's own records every week. Click "Load Permits" below to see this week's list.
🌐 What's real here, in plain terms
This list is real — it comes straight from the City of Houston, updated once a week (not second-by-second). It only gives us the property's address, not the owner's name, which is completely fine: mail addressed simply to "Property Owner" reaches the right house and is standard practice for this kind of outreach. On the rare property where public county tax records clearly show a real owner's name, your mailer letter will use that name instead — otherwise it stays addressed to "Property Owner," never a guess. Where you see a 🏛️ REAL HCAD DATA badge, the home value shown comes directly from Harris County's own official property records. Neighborhood names below (River Oaks, The Heights, etc.) are general area groupings, not exact legal boundaries.

A Simple Letter You Can Copy Yourself
The "Create Mailer Letters" button above already writes a personal letter for every address you select, automatically. This box is just a backup — a simple letter you can copy and paste yourself if you'd rather handle it another way.
Property Owner

Hello,

My name is [FOUNDER_NAME]. I'm reaching out from [COMPANY_NAME], serving [SERVICE_AREA].

You can reach me directly at [PHONE] or [EMAIL]. We'd be glad to answer any questions about your property — no obligation, no pitch.

[FOUNDER_NAME] · [COMPANY_NAME]
Why We Suggest Mail Instead of Phone Calls or Texts
Calling or texting people using public permit records, without their permission first, can break federal telemarketing law and lead to real fines. A printed letter sent to their address doesn't have that risk — which is exactly why this tool only gives you addresses, not phone numbers.

"Likely due" is a helpful estimate based on typical service life, not a guarantee anything is actually broken.
